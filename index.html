What research has he done?

Five papers, four of them accepted. Each one opens in full.

Interpretable Multilingual Attribution of Frontier LLMs

Paper · IEEE BigData 2026 · Phoenix, 17 Dec

Best Paper Finalistone of 12, from 130 accepted papers

Can you still tell which AI model wrote an essay once it leaves English? Yes: an interpretable classifier names the model 59–72% of the time in all seven languages, while the models themselves manage 20–25%.

How

21 features from Universal Dependencies parses, on 840 essays from five frontier models. They transfer between languages better than character n-grams (49% against 41%).

Authors

Adrian Erlikhman, Michael Tarekegn, Philo Juang · co-first author

Curated to Win: Certifying Reward-Model and Judge Selection Against Evaluation-Set Curation

Paper · ARRSI @ AACL-IJCNLP 2026 · Hengqin, 10 Nov

Accepted, sole authorARRSI Workshop, AACL-IJCNLP 2026

How few test items does it take to change which reward model wins? RewardBench’s leader survives the removal of any 11 of its 2,985 items; removing the right 12 makes the runner-up the winner.

How

A curation certificate, solved as linear programs over 118 RewardBench and 188 RewardBench 2 models; on RewardBench 2 the leader survives any 42 items.

Author

Adrian Erlikhman, sole author

Whose Line Is It Anyway? Auditing Self- and Other-Recognition in LLM Judges

Paper · preprint

PreprintarXiv forthcoming

Do LLM judges really recognize their own writing? The usual test flags 15 of 37 cases; 8 survive a false-alarm control, and none beats the judge’s strongest single peer.

How

Nine judges, 1,270 texts and 19,000+ judgments, plus two controls: how often a judge names itself on text it didn’t write, and how often its peers name it.

Authors

Adrian Erlikhman, Michael Tarekegn, Philo Juang · co-first author

Does Portfolio Optimization Beat Splitting Evenly?

Paper · Oxford Journal of Student Scholarship

Accepted with revisionsOxford Journal of Student Scholarship

Does textbook portfolio optimization beat splitting your money evenly? No: the even split won 74% of 250 simulations, with a Sharpe ratio of 0.70 against Markowitz’s 0.51.

How

250 simulations of a factor model calibrated to real market data. Given the true means the optimizer nearly wins, so estimating expected returns is the whole problem.

Authors

Adrian Erlikhman, Ryan Erlikhman · co-first author

How Robust Are the Legatum Prosperity Index Rankings? A Monte Carlo and Sensitivity Analysis of Weighting, Normalization, and Aggregation

Paper · Journal of High School Science

Accepted with revisionsJournal of High School Science

How much do global prosperity rankings depend on arbitrary choices? The ranking holds as a whole (median rank correlation 0.99), but a mid-table country’s 90% rank interval spans a median of 36 places.

How

Every published score for 167 countries reproduced exactly, then 10,000 draws under three weight priors and a 131,072-run Sobol’ design. New result: rank leverage, c²(1 − ρ²).

Authors

Adrian Erlikhman, Ryan Erlikhman · co-first author
