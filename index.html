What research have you done?

Five papers, four of them accepted. Click any one for the details.

  1. Interpretable Multilingual Attribution of Frontier LLMs

    IEEE BigData 2026, High School Symposium · Phoenix, 17 Dec

    Our classifier names which frontier AI wrote an essay 59–72% of the time in all seven languages, from English to Hindi, while the AI models themselves do little better than chance (20–25%). Built on 840 essays from GPT-5.5, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini, Grok and DeepSeek, it still works after machine translation. One of 12 Best Paper finalists from 130 accepted papers.

    Adrian Erlikhman, Michael Tarekegn, Philo Juang

    Best Paper Finalistaccepted

  2. Curated to Win: Certifying Reward-Model and Judge Selection Against Evaluation-Set Curation

    ARRSI Workshop @ AACL-IJCNLP 2026 · Hengqin, 10 Nov

    A certificate for when an AI leaderboard can be trusted. Automated research and self-improving AI pipelines pick which reward model or judge to trust by its score on a test set, and that set is often hand-picked. Across 306 models on RewardBench and RewardBench 2, the certificate shows RewardBench’s leader survives losing any 11 of its 2,985 questions, but removing the right 12 crowns the runner-up.

    Adrian Erlikhman

    Acceptedsole author

  3. Whose Line Is It Anyway? Auditing Self- and Other-Recognition in LLM Judges

    Preprint · code and data on GitHub

    Can AI judges recognize their own writing? The standard test says yes in 15 of 37 cases; with two controls we added, not one beats the judge’s strongest single peer. Nine judges, 1,270 texts, 19,000+ judgments. Claude picks out its own essays 33 times in 38, but GPT picks out Claude’s 32 times.

    Adrian Erlikhman, Michael Tarekegn, Philo Juang

    PreprintarXiv forthcoming

  4. Does Portfolio Optimization Beat Splitting Evenly?

    Oxford Journal of Student Scholarship

    Splitting money evenly beat the portfolio that Nobel-winning Markowitz theory calls optimal in 74% of 250 market simulations, worth about 3 points of return a year at the same risk (Sharpe ratio 0.70 against 0.51). The culprit is estimating expected returns: given the true means, the optimizer is nearly optimal.

    Adrian Erlikhman, Ryan Erlikhman

    Accepted with revisions

  5. How Robust Are the Legatum Prosperity Index Rankings? A Monte Carlo and Sensitivity Analysis of Weighting, Normalization, and Aggregation

    Journal of High School Science

    We rebuilt a ranking of 167 countries exactly, then stress-tested it with 30,000 alternative weightings and a 131,072-run sensitivity analysis. The ranking holds as a whole, but a mid-table country can move across a median 36-place range. We also derive rank leverage, a formula for how much each weight can reorder countries.

    Adrian Erlikhman, Ryan Erlikhman

    Accepted with revisions
